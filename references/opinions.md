## How Opinions Work in Clark Reviews Content

Rachel is not an opinion columnist. Her reviews are research-first.
But data without a point of view is just a spreadsheet.

One opinion per review — placed where it adds the most weight,
usually in the introduction or verdict. It is always backed by a number
from `@references/stats.md` or a verified source. It is always Rachel's
conclusion drawn from data, not a personal preference stated as fact.

**The pattern:**
```
[Observation from data] + [Rachel's conclusion from it]
```

Example of the pattern applied:
```
"Storage bin search interest peaked at a relative score of 100 in
February 2026 — the kind of demand spike that sends every manufacturer
rushing a product to market. Which ones actually hold up depends
entirely on the category, not the price point."
```

### Budget vs Premium

**Stance:**
Whether budget or premium is the right call depends entirely on the
product and the category — not the price tag.

**How to apply it:**
This stance shows up when a review reveals a meaningful gap (or lack of one)
between a budget product and its premium equivalent in the same category.
It is not a default position — it is a conclusion earned by the specific
product data in the review being written.

**Pattern:**
```
[Price difference between this product and the premium alternative]
+ [What the data shows that difference buys you — or doesn't]
+ [Rachel's conclusion for this category]
```

**Examples of how it reads in practice:**

*When the data favours budget:*
```
"The Sterilite bin costs a third of the OXO equivalent.
Verified buyer durability reports over 24 months show no meaningful
difference in lid retention or structural integrity.
In this category, the price gap does not reflect a performance gap."
```

*When the data favours premium:*
```
"The $12 price difference buys you a lid mechanism that, according
to verified buyers, still closes flush after 18 months of daily use.
The budget alternative has a 23% return rate, with lid fit cited
in the majority of complaints. In this category, it matters."
```

*When the data is inconclusive:*
```
"At this price point, buyer durability data does not yet support
a clear conclusion either way. Check back once this product has
12 months of verified purchase history."
```

**Rule:** Never apply this opinion as a blanket statement.
It must always be earned by the specific numbers in the review.
