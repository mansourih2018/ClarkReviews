# stories.md — Clark Reviews
# Situational Story Guide

> Read this file before writing any review.
> One story per review maximum — placed in the introduction only.
> Stories are not personal testing anecdotes.
> They are universal domestic situations the reader has almost certainly
> lived through themselves.
> The agent generates a suitable story per review using the guidelines below.

---

## What These Stories Are

A story in a Clark Reviews introduction is a **moment of recognition** —
a situation so common that the reader thinks "yes, that's exactly it"
before the product is even named.

It is not:
- A personal testing claim ("I used this for two weeks and found...")
- A made-up anecdote attributed to Rachel
- A customer testimonial
- A fictional scenario with characters and dialogue

It is:
- A universally relatable domestic situation
- Written in the second person ("your pantry", "the drawer", "your kitchen")
  or the impersonal third ("the kind of shelf that...")
- Tied directly to the problem the product being reviewed claims to solve
- One to three sentences maximum
- Followed immediately by what the product promises to do about it

---

## The Structure

```
[Universal situation — the problem in concrete detail]
[What the product claims to solve it]
[Verdict direction — what the research shows]
```

**Example:**
```
"The pantry shelf where things go in and do not come back out in any
organised way — where you own three identical can openers because
the first two became unlocatable. The OXO Expandable Drawer Organiser
claims to fix that. The specs suggest it mostly does."
```

---

## Rules

- **Second person or impersonal** — "your drawer", "the cabinet", never "my drawer"
- **Concrete, not vague** — name the specific frustration, not a general category
- **One to three sentences** — if it needs more, it is not tight enough
- **Connects directly to the product** — the situation must be the exact
  problem this product is designed for
- **No exaggeration for comic effect** — dry observation only
- **No resolution in the story** — the product provides the resolution, not the story
- **One per review** — if the story is written, no further anecdotes appear anywhere
  else in that review

## Story Patterns by Category

Use these as starting points. Adapt to the specific product being reviewed.

---

### Air Fryers

```
Pattern A — the counterspace negotiation:
"The kitchen counter where every appliance is in permanent competition
for the same eighteen inches of usable space. [Product] promises to
earn its place. The footprint specs suggest it might."

Pattern B — the cooking habit mismatch:
"The oven that heats the whole kitchen to cook one portion.
[Product] claims to handle the same job in a fraction of the time
and without the thermal event. Buyer data on cook times backs that up,
with one caveat."

Pattern C — the previous purchase regret:
"The appliance bought on the strength of a video that looked convincing
and stopped working before the warranty expired. [Product] has been on
the market long enough that its failure patterns are visible in the
return data. Here is what they show."
```

---

### Storage

```
Pattern A — the retrieval problem:
"The cabinet where things are stored but not findable —
where the act of putting something away is also the act of losing it.
[Product] is designed for exactly this. Whether it delivers depends
on the dimensions of your shelves."

Pattern B — the under-used space:
"The gap under the bed, behind the door, above the fridge —
space that exists but does not function. [Product] claims to change
the arithmetic. The specs on capacity and stackability suggest it can,
within limits."

Pattern C — the accumulation problem:
"The point at which the number of storage containers exceeds the
available storage space for them. [Product] promises to consolidate
that. Verified buyers report it does — until you need the lid."
```

---

### Organisation

```
Pattern A — the drawer situation:
"The kitchen drawer that functions as a horizontal filing system
for everything that has no other home. [Product] promises to impose
a structure on it. Whether the internal dimensions match a real
kitchen drawer is the first question the specs answer."

Pattern B — the visible chaos:
"The pantry that looks organised in the first week after a clear-out
and returns to its natural state within a month. [Product] claims
to make the organised state the default. Buyer retention data at
six months is the relevant number here."

Pattern C — the incomplete system:
"The organisation system bought in one size that does not fit the
shelf it was bought for. [Product] adjusts. The adjustment range
in the specs covers most standard kitchen cabinets, with exceptions
noted below."
```

---

### Cleaning

```
Pattern A — the effort-to-result gap:
"The floor that looks the same after mopping as it did before —
where the process of cleaning it is indistinguishable in outcome
from not cleaning it. [Product] makes a specific claim about what
it removes and from what surfaces. Verified buyer data on grout
and hard floors is where that claim either holds or doesn't."

Pattern B — the product that ran out:
"The cleaning spray that was nearly empty last time and is now
definitely empty. A category where the difference between products
that work and products that smell like they work is almost entirely
in the buyer complaint data."

Pattern C — the appliance cleaning avoidance:
"The appliance cleaned less often than it should be because the
process of cleaning it is unreasonably involved. [Product] claims
to simplify it. The manual is three pages, which is a reasonable sign."
```

---

## How the Agent Generates a Story

For each review, select the pattern from the relevant category that
most closely matches the specific product problem. Then:

1. Replace `[Product]` with the actual product name
2. Replace generic references with the specific feature or claim
   being reviewed (e.g. "stackability", "grout performance", "footprint")
3. Replace the placeholder verdict line with the actual verdict direction
   drawn from the research
4. Trim to three sentences or fewer
5. Confirm it reads in second person or impersonal — never first person

The result should feel like it was written for this specific product,
not pulled from a template.

## What a Finished Story Looks Like

**Product:** Bissell Little Green Portable Cleaner
**Category:** Cleaning — Pattern C (appliance cleaning avoidance)

```
"The upholstery stain left long enough that it has become part of
the furniture. Bissell's Little Green claims to lift it with a
handheld machine small enough to store in a cabinet and simple
enough to use without consulting a manual. Buyer data on set-in
stains is where that claim is either confirmed or quietly not."
```

---

**Product:** IRIS USA Stack & Pull Storage Box
**Category:** Storage — Pattern B (under-used space)

```
"The space under the bed that currently stores things by the method
of sliding them under and hoping. IRIS USA's Stack & Pull boxes
claim to bring a retrievable system to that space.
The lid mechanism is where most of the verified complaints live."
```
