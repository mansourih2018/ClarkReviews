# Section 2 — Product-Level Research Sources

---

## What Product Stats Are

Product-level stats are specific to the item being reviewed.
They are gathered fresh during Step 5 of `/write-review` and
documented in the review's frontmatter and body copy.
They do not live in this file — they live in the review itself.

Examples of product-level stats:
```
- Return rate for [Product] on Amazon: X%
- Most cited complaint across verified buyers: [specific issue]
- % of 1-star reviews mentioning [specific flaw]: X%
- Average lifespan reported by long-term buyers: X months
- Buyer satisfaction at 6 months vs 12 months: X% vs X%
- Number of verified reviews at time of writing: X
```

---

## Where to Find Product Stats

Research these sources in order during Step 5 of `/write-review`.

### 1. Amazon Verified Reviews
**What to look for:**
- Filter: "Verified Purchase" only — ignore unverified
- Sort: "Most Recent" to catch emerging issues
- Sort: "Most Critical" to find complaint patterns
- Read the 1-star and 2-star reviews in full — return reasons
  and failure points are documented here
- Note: total review count, average rating, and date of earliest reviews
  (product age affects how much data is available)

**What to record:**
```
- Most common complaint (named in X% of critical reviews)
- Most common praise (named in X% of positive reviews)
- Any recurring safety or quality issue
- Whether complaints cluster at a specific timeframe ("stopped working after 6 months")
```

---

### 2. Amazon Q&A Section
**What to look for:**
- Questions about fit, compatibility, and durability
- Brand responses — whether the manufacturer engages and what they say
- Repeated questions signal a gap between the listing and reality

---

### 3. Reddit
**Subreddits to check:**
- `r/BuyItForLife` — durability and long-term ownership reports
- `r/HomeImprovement` — installation and real-home fit
- `r/organization` — real use cases and complaints
- `r/Frugal` — value-for-money perspective
- Product-specific subreddits where they exist

**What to look for:**
- Unprompted owner reports (not in response to a review prompt)
- Failure timelines ("mine lasted 14 months before the lid cracked")
- Comparisons to alternatives from people who have owned both

---

### 4. RTINGS.com
**Use for:** Any product with measurable performance specs —
vacuums, air purifiers, small appliances with wattage or output claims.

**What to look for:**
- Lab-measured performance vs manufacturer claim
- Score breakdowns by individual criterion
- How the product compares to category benchmarks

---

### 5. Consumer Reports
**Use for:** Products where failure rate and owner satisfaction
data is available.

**What to look for:**
- Owner satisfaction scores at 1 year and 3 years
- Predicted reliability rating
- Repair or replacement frequency

---

### 6. Manufacturer Spec Sheet
**Always check — never skip.**

**What to verify:**
- Exact dimensions (length × width × height)
- Materials and coatings (with full names, not marketing terms)
- Warranty terms (what is and is not covered, and for how long)
- Country of manufacture
- Any certifications claimed (UL, NSF, PFOA-free, BPA-free — verify these are third-party certified, not self-declared)

---

### 7. YouTube Long-Term Reviews
**Use for:** Products where visual inspection of wear and failure matters —
storage bins, cleaning tools, organisers.

**What to look for:**
- Videos titled "X months later" or "long-term update"
- Honest assessments from creators who are not sponsored for that specific video
- Visual evidence of wear, discolouration, cracking, or mechanism failure

---

## How to Document Product Stats in a Review

When a product stat is worth citing in copy, format it as follows:

**In the review body:**
```
"X% of critical verified reviews cite [specific issue] as the
reason for return — a pattern consistent enough to note."

"At [X] months on the market with [X,000] verified reviews,
the complaint data is substantial enough to draw conclusions."

"The manufacturer claims [specific spec]. The product manual
confirms this. Buyer reports on [related performance point]
are consistent with that figure."
```

**Flagging unverifiable stats:**
If a claimed figure cannot be verified from a named source, do not use it.
Write around it:
```
"The manufacturer states [claim]. This could not be independently
verified at time of writing."
```

---

## Annual Stat Review

Check every stat in Section 1 against its review date once per year.
If the source has published updated figures, replace the old stat.
If the source is no longer available, remove the stat.
Never leave an outdated stat in the file — the agent will use it.
